# Working findings: a replicable staffing forecast from one entry counter

Aim: results that a less crowded site in Madinah could reproduce with one entry counter, a public holiday calendar and weather data. Weather effects use JMA station observations (`20261012_weather_jma.md`); `20261011_weather_openmeteo.md` is the same analysis on Open-Meteo, the global source we would use in Madinah. Cross-site patterns are in `20261011_cross_site_patterns.md`.

## What holds so far

1. **Weather sensitivity rises with site exposure.** Each 10 mm of daytime rain changes daily counts by:
   - Fukui Station (transit hub): -3% (±3)
   - Tojinbo (coastal cliff): -9% (±5)
   - Rainbow Line (mountain toll road, cars): -40% (±20)

   Open-Meteo gives the same ordering (-1%, -15%, -37%).

   Sun and warmth push the same way. For Madinah, the transferable result is the ordering, not the coefficients. The weather variable that matters there will differ (heat rather than rain), and the analysis has to be rerun on local data.
2. **Weather helps forecasts only at exposed sites.** At three days ahead, Rainbow Line WAPE falls from 38.5% (calendar only) to 33.9% with weather (31.3% with Open-Meteo). At the station, weather adds nothing.
3. **Busy-day warnings three days ahead are already usable.**
   - Fukui Station: AUC 0.91 to 0.92
   - Tojinbo: AUC 0.91 to 0.93
   - Rainbow Line, "car park 2 in use", the trigger for the shuttle and extra staff: precision 0.86 and recall 0.64 with JMA weather, against 0.60 and 0.55 without
4. **A practical target of 70% of days within ±30% three days ahead** is met:
   - Fukui Station: 90% of days
   - Tojinbo: 70% with JMA weather
   - Rainbow Line: 54%, so not yet
5. **Car park 2 starts filling once car park 1 passes about 300 cars a day.** Plates count gate passages, not occupancy.
6. **Busy days are driven by visitors from outside Fukui.** Kansai and Chubu make up 55% of plates on busy days, against 33% on quiet days.
7. **Counts outside opening hours are small** (0.7% of plates), which bounds noise from staff vehicles.

## With real forecasts (scan03)

Models trained on observed weather but predicting with the Open-Meteo forecast issued 1 or 3 days earlier keep almost all of the gain:

- Rainbow Line, 3 days ahead: WAPE 38.5% (calendar) to 32.6% (forecast weather), change -5.9 points [95% CI -8.9, -3.0], Diebold-Mariano p < 0.001
- Tojinbo, 1 day ahead: -1.8 points [-3.3, -0.4], p = 0.015; at 3 days the gain is not significant
- Fukui Station: no gain, as expected for a sheltered transit site
- Car park 2 warning 3 days ahead: precision 0.80 against 0.60 without weather (AUC 0.88)

## Planning horizon (scan04, run on the seminar box)

- At Rainbow Line, forecast weather cuts WAPE significantly for leads of 1 to 5 days (5 to 6 points, DM p ≤ 0.001). The gain fades at 6 to 7 days, as the weather forecast itself loses skill. That gives a natural planning window for staff rosters.
- Gradient boosting beats ridge by 1 to 2 points at the exposed sites (Tojinbo from 2 days, Rainbow Line at every lead), but is worse at Fukui Station.
- Busy-day AUC stays at or above 0.85 up to 5 days ahead at every site. The car park 2 warning holds at 0.86 to 0.92 for all 7 leads.

## Caveats before any of this goes in the paper

- The busy threshold is set on training data. Because demand grew, 30 to 40% of test days count as busy, not 20%.
- Plate counts are cars, not people. Occupancy is unknown, and the camera cannot tell a microbus from a car.
- Cold start (how much history a new camera needs) is covered separately. See the paper repository for that analysis.
